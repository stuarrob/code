\begin{abstract}
Long-only smart-beta ETF strategy operated against a live IB Gateway account. Selects 30 positions from a 599-ticker hand-curated universe (leveraged / inverse / commodity / currency / volatility excluded) by weighted geometric mean of four AQR-canonical factors: momentum 35\%, quality 30\%, low-volatility 20\%, value 15\%. Exponential rank weighting, 2\%--15\% position bounds, 10\% trailing stops, bimonthly rebalance with 5\% drift trigger. \textbf{Multi-regime validated over 15.5 years (2011--2026): CAGR +13.0\%, Sharpe +0.51, MaxDD $-$37\%; beats SPY same-period CAGR by $\sim$0.9\%/yr at comparable drawdown.} This document is the operator's reading surface; the code in \texttt{src/} is authoritative for behaviour, this document for intent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Strategy Mechanics}
\label{sec:mechanics}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Universe Selection}

The strategy runs on a hand-curated smart-beta universe declared in \texttt{src/data\_collection/comprehensive\_etf\_list.py} and enforced by \texttt{src.data\_collection.etf\_filters.filter\_universe(use\_curated=True)} in both the live pipeline and the multi-regime backtest diagnostic. Constructed once, revisited annually.

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Hand-picked categories: US broad / mid / small, growth / value tilts, sector, dividend, international developed / emerging, bonds broad / credit, REIT, factor-multi.
    \item Excluded categories: commodities, currency single-country, volatility products, thematic-single-narrative (cannabis, cannabis-only clean energy, etc.).
    \item Excluded structure: leveraged / inverse / short (2x, 3x, ProShares Ultra, Direxion Daily) removed by enumerated list plus name-pattern matching.
    \item History requirement: $\geq$252 trading days of price data before scoring at each rebalance date.
    \item Result: \textbf{599 curated tickers} eligible for scoring at the 2026-07 snapshot. The screen is applied identically in the diagnostic and live pipeline so that backtest results simulate live behaviour.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Factor Framework --- What the Four Factors Are and Why}

The four factors below are the AQR-canonical smart-beta four: momentum, quality, low-volatility, value. They were chosen because they are the four with the largest and most robust empirical premia over the widest range of markets and time periods studied in the academic literature. AQR chose these because after decades of empirical work they are the return sources that survive out-of-sample testing, transaction costs, and factor-crowding. The strategy does not attempt to identify novel factors.

\subsubsection{Momentum (target 35\% weight)}

\begin{equation}
\text{Momentum}_i = \frac{P_{i,t-21} - P_{i,t-252}}{P_{i,t-252}}
\end{equation}

12-month return, with the most recent 21 days omitted. Winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentile.

\textbf{Why:} Assets that have outperformed over the prior year tend to continue outperforming over the next year. The 1-month skip avoids the short-term reversal effect that would contaminate the trend signal. Established by Jegadeesh \& Titman (1993); the skip refinement from Novy-Marx (2012).

\textbf{Why AQR uses it:} The most-studied, most-robust factor after value, and the largest-premium factor in the last decade. Works across equity, futures, and FX. Fails during trend reversals (2022 tech drawdown, 2000 dot-com peak).

\subsubsection{Quality (target 30\% weight)}

Composite of three z-score-normalised components on 252-day windows:

\begin{align}
\text{Sharpe Ratio} &= \frac{\mu_r - r_f}{\sigma_r} \sqrt{252} \quad (40\% \text{ weight}) \\
\text{Drawdown Resilience} &= -1 \times \text{MaxDD} \quad (30\% \text{ weight}) \\
\text{Return Stability} &= -1 \times \sigma_r \sqrt{252} \quad (30\% \text{ weight})
\end{align}

\textbf{Why:} Assets that produce stable, high risk-adjusted returns tend to continue doing so. Established by Asness, Frazzini \& Pedersen (2019).

\textbf{Why AQR uses it:} Quality captures the defensive-equity premium. Correlated with low-volatility but not identical --- quality picks up profitability and stability of \emph{returns}, low-vol picks up stability of \emph{prices}. Both belong in the portfolio because they hedge different aspects of stress.

\subsubsection{Low-Volatility (target 20\% weight)}

\begin{equation}
\text{Volatility}_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_{60d,i} \times \sqrt{252}}
\end{equation}

Inverse of annualised realised volatility over 60 trading days. Higher score for lower-volatility ETFs.

\textbf{Why:} The low-volatility anomaly --- lower-volatility assets have historically produced higher risk-adjusted returns than higher-volatility assets, contradicting the CAPM. Baker, Bradley \& Wurgler (2011).

\textbf{Why AQR uses it:} Two reasons. First, the low-vol premium is real and persistent. Second, low-vol allocations reduce portfolio-level whipsaw, which matters more than arithmetic-mean metrics suggest in a compounding portfolio (see \S\ref{sec:weights}).

\subsubsection{Value (target 15\% weight)}

\begin{equation}
\text{Value}_i = 0.60 \cdot z(\text{DividendYield}_i) + 0.40 \cdot z(-\text{ExpenseRatio}_i)
\end{equation}

\textbf{What each component contributes.} Dividend yield is the canonical fund-level value tilt for ETFs --- funds like SCHD, VYM, HDV, and VTV that hold value-tilted holdings publish materially higher trailing yields than growth-oriented funds like VUG, VGT, and QQQ. It is a real cross-sectional signal, not a fee preference. Expense ratio contributes the cost-efficiency component: for two funds with similar yield, the cheaper one is a better long-run compounder. Both components are $z$-score-normalised on the current cross-section before the blend.

\textbf{Data source.} Real per-fund dividend yield is computed as $\texttt{lastDividend} / \texttt{price}$ from Financial Modeling Prep's \texttt{stable/profile} endpoint. Real per-fund expense ratio comes from the \texttt{stable/etf/info} endpoint. Both are cached weekly to \texttt{\textasciitilde/trade\_data/ETFTrader/processed/etf\_fundamentals.parquet} by \texttt{scripts/refresh\_fundamentals.py}. Rankings verified on 2026-07-10: SCHD 1.04, HDV 0.81, VYM 0.56, VTV 0.38, VOO $-0.04$, VUG $-0.39$, VGT $-0.50$, ARKK $-1.81$ --- the expected value ordering.

\textbf{Residual gap.} Fund-level weighted P/E and P/B are still absent. The FMP endpoints that publish these (\texttt{key-metrics-ttm}, \texttt{ratios-ttm}) aggregate 10-K filings and return zero rows for ETFs at any subscription tier because ETFs do not file 10-Ks. Adding true weighted-average P/E would require holdings-level aggregation via \texttt{etf/holdings} (Ultimate tier) or a Morningstar-class source. Documented as an open enhancement in \S\ref{sec:testing}. Current implementation delivers approximately 60\% of the intended value composite.

\textbf{Why AQR uses value:} It is the oldest and most-studied factor (Fama \& French 1993). Tends to work when growth and momentum break (2000--2002, 2022). Yield-based value is a defensible, slowly-varying subset of the full academic tilt.

\subsection{Factor Integration}

\begin{equation}
\text{Score}_i = \text{Mom}_i^{0.35} \times \text{Qual}_i^{0.30} \times \text{Vol}_i^{0.20} \times \text{Val}_i^{0.15}
\end{equation}

Each factor is first converted to a percentile rank in [0,1]. The integrated score is the weighted geometric mean of ranks.

\textbf{Why geometric mean, not arithmetic:} Geometric-mean integration penalises weakness on any one factor. An ETF in the 90th percentile on three factors but the 20th on one ranks below an ETF in the 70th percentile on all four. This ``multi-factor consistency'' requirement --- explicitly the key insight from AQR's factor-integration research --- rejects one-trick candidates in favour of broadly-strong candidates. Arithmetic integration would allow one strong factor to compensate for weakness on the others.

\subsection{Portfolio Construction}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Score every ETF in the filtered universe.
    \item Rank descending.
    \item Take the top 30 (raised from 20 on 2026-07-10 --- see Change Log).
    \item Assign exponential rank weights: $w_i \propto e^{-\text{rank}_i / 30}$, normalised to sum to 1.
    \item Apply weight bounds: minimum 2\%, maximum 15\%.
\end{enumerate}

Exponential weighting expresses conviction --- the top-ranked position gets meaningfully more weight than the thirtieth --- while the bounds prevent single-position concentration. The alternative optimiser (\texttt{OPTIMIZER\_TYPE = "mvo"}) is available in the code but not the live default.

\subsection{Rebalancing}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Scheduled: bimonthly ($\sim$6 times per year).
    \item Drift-triggered: if any position's actual weight deviates from target by $>$5\%, rebalance.
    \item In practice, drift triggers dominate; scheduled rebalances rarely fire independently.
\end{enumerate}

Drift threshold is deliberately wide. Tighter thresholds cause more trading on smaller deviations --- textbook whipsaw. See \S\ref{sec:principles}.

\subsection{Risk Management}

Every entry BUY places a 10\% trailing stop, GTC, on the position. Trailing stops ratchet up as the position appreciates. On any 10\% pullback from the running high, the stop triggers.

Entry stop-loss of 12\% (a bit wider than the trailing stop) accommodates initial-position volatility before the trailing high-water mark establishes.

Cash reserve of \$30{,}000 is held unallocated (lowered from \$70{,}000 on 2026-07-10 to free capital for the widened 30-position basket). This is a static allocation, not a regime-responsive hedge; the T2.1 regime overlay in progress will replace the static reserve with a dynamic risk-off multiplier.

\subsection{Explicit Assumptions}

The strategy assumes:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Factor premia (momentum, quality, low-vol, value) will continue to exist over the holding horizon. They may attenuate but do not disappear.
    \item The rank structure of the universe changes slowly enough that monthly rebalancing captures the signal without significant slippage.
    \item Retail transaction costs at IB ($\sim$5 bps effective) are small enough not to erode the premium.
    \item Long-only holds; no short exposure to hedge market drawdown.
    \item No leverage.
    \item Trailing stops execute at or near their trigger prices. Gap-down scenarios (weekend news, halt reopens) are unhedged.
    \item Position selection ignores tax lot-level implications. Tax-lot management is out of scope.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Design Principles}
\label{sec:principles}

Three non-negotiable filters govern every future proposal:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Deterministic decides.} Every number reaching the broker comes from code in \texttt{src/}. No LLM output influences a size or weight.
    \item \textbf{No magical thinking on knobs.} Every parameter traces to research or a repo-recorded backtest. Knobs whose provenance is intuition go on the research backlog.
    \item \textbf{Slowly varying only.} Whipsaw is evidence of overfitting, not alpha. Long lookbacks are preferred over short. Drift thresholds err high. Regime signals (when added) require hysteresis and minimum dwell time. Factor timing is rejected on principle.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Factor Weights}

The weight vector is momentum $=$ 35\%, quality $=$ 30\%, low-volatility $=$ 20\%, value $=$ 15\%. This is an \emph{informed prior}, deliberately regularised away from any in-sample optimum.

\subsubsection{Why not the in-sample winner?}

The July~2026 diagnostic (grid search over the three-factor simplex, momentum/quality/low-vol, on the cached 2021--2026 history) produced in-sample winners uniformly momentum-heavy: 80--90\% momentum, 0--30\% quality, 10--20\% low-vol. Sharpe up to 1.30 on the 3-year window versus $\approx$0.96 for the current policy.

\textbf{This finding was deliberately not acted on.} Three reasons:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Single-regime evidence.} 2021--2026 was overwhelmingly momentum-friendly (post-COVID rally, mega-cap tech, AI narrative). Both the 5-year and 3-year windows overlap and sample the same regime. This is not an independent invariance test.
    \item \textbf{Non-ergodicity.} Equity returns are multiplicative. For a single investor on a single path, the arithmetic mean of returns diverges from the time-average growth rate they actually experience (Peters 2019). A 50\% drawdown requires 100\% subsequent gain to recover, not 50\%. In-sample-Sharpe optimisation understates this asymmetry. Factors that reduce drawdown variance (quality, low-vol) buy protection against the multiplicative losses a single-path investor most needs to avoid. These are insurance premia, not underperforming factors.
    \item \textbf{Confirmation bias.} The operator is explicitly on record as most concerned about an AI/tech valuation regime unwind. The mechanical winner (90\% momentum) is exactly the exposure that would be worst affected in that scenario. Adopting the in-sample winner would be confirming a bias against the operator's stated risk view.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{The Constraint Framework}

The 35/30/20/15 vector satisfies three constraints:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Ordering: momentum $>$ quality $>$ low-vol $>$ value.} Reflects the ordering of the empirical premia in the AQR literature and the compounding effect that the value implementation is currently a low-fee proxy rather than a true value factor.
    \item \textbf{No factor below 10\%.} A factor below 10\% receives such a small weight in the weighted geometric mean that it fails to discriminate between candidates. The multi-factor-consistency requirement collapses to a three-factor screen.
    \item \textbf{No factor above 40\%.} A factor above 40\% dominates the integrated score to the point that single-factor selection would produce nearly the same portfolio. This defeats the multi-factor rationale and re-introduces single-factor tail risk.
\end{enumerate}

Within those constraints the exact values are round numbers chosen for auditability. They can be reproduced in one sentence to a future maintainer: ``momentum gets the largest weight but capped, quality gets the second-largest, low-vol gets a solid third to hedge whipsaw, value gets the smallest until we fix the proxy.''

\subsubsection{When To Revise}

Revise the weights when at least one of the following is available:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Backfilled history to pre-2020 that permits a non-overlapping regime comparison (2011--2015 vs 2021--2026).
    \item A fund-level P/E or P/B signal added to the value composite (yield + expense ratio blend committed 2026-07-10 partially satisfies this; full P/E/P/B would fully satisfy).
    \item A deliberate operator-declared factor-level sentiment override, committed to \texttt{configs/etf\_smart\_beta.toml} with a dated research note.
\end{itemize}

\section{Testing and Validation}
\label{sec:testing}
%% ============================================================

The strategy has been subjected to a systematic multi-regime backtest with pre-registered acceptance criteria. This section replaces the prior ``Current Flaws / adversarial review'' framing: the identified concerns have either been addressed, tested and dismissed, or explicitly accepted after considered judgement. The purpose of this section is to establish, with numbers, that the operating policy is defensible.

\subsection{Test methodology}

Full write-up: \texttt{docs/research/2026-07\_multi\_regime\_results.md} and companion CSV / plots. Headline discipline:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Universe:} 599-ticker curated smart-beta universe (\texttt{comprehensive\_etf\_list.py} categories minus commodities / currency / volatility products; leveraged and inverse ETFs excluded via \texttt{filter\_universe} in the live pipeline and diagnostic alike). No filter-and-hope on niche exotica.
    \item \textbf{Data window:} 2011-01-28 to 2026-04-30, 3\,904 trading days ($\sim$15.5 years). Covers EU crisis 2011, taper tantrum 2013, China 2015--16, 2018 Q4, COVID 2020, 2022 rate cycle. Deep enough for a multi-regime verdict; not deep enough to claim ergodicity.
    \item \textbf{Windows:} results reported for full period, trailing 5-year, trailing 3-year. Robustness of a config is judged by min-metric across the three windows, not the peak in any one.
    \item \textbf{Pre-registered criteria:} weight change proposed only if a nearby config beats the prior by $\geq 0.10$ Sharpe on the min-across-windows metric. Regime overlay enabled live only if Sortino improves \emph{and} CAGR loss $\leq 2$\%. Clustering enabled only if MaxDD improves $\geq 2$\% and CAGR loss $\leq 1.5$\% across $\geq 2$ windows. Written before results known; not retro-fitted.
    \item \textbf{Transaction cost model:} 5 bps applied to turnover per rebalance. A retail-plausible estimate; live cost is expected to be modestly higher.
\end{itemize}

The 70\% momentum concentration wins narrowly (hinting momentum was the strongest single factor 2011--2026), but the multi-factor prior is preferred on drawdown-adjusted terms (Sortino) and on the design-principle grounds (\S\ref{sec:weights}: insurance premium, non-ergodicity).

\textbf{Verdict: retain 35 / 30 / 20 / 15.} The prior is not the empirical peak but is inside the plateau. No change proposed to \texttt{configs/etf\_smart\_beta.toml}.

\subsection{Sober response to prior adversarial concerns}

A short, item-by-item accounting of what has been done since the earlier ``Current Flaws'' review:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Value factor was a fee proxy (T1.1).} Closed 2026-07-10. Real per-fund dividend yield + expense ratio blend from FMP Premium. Rankings verified (SCHD, HDV, VYM at the top; VUG, VGT, ARKK at the bottom). Fund-level P/E and P/B remain absent because no FMP tier publishes them for ETFs; the current implementation delivers the yield-based subset of the academic value composite. Not a fake factor any more.
    \item \textbf{Universe was uncurated (implicit).} Now explicit. 599 hand-picked tickers, leverage and inverse enumerated, commodity / currency / volatility categories excluded. The diagnostic and live pipeline use the same screen. Prior backtests showing $-68$\% and $-52$\% drawdowns were artefacts of leveraged ETFs slipping through incomplete filters; the true baseline is $-36.9$\%, in line with SPY.
    \item \textbf{Correlation blindness (T2.2).} Tested empirically. On multi-regime data, correlation clustering does not improve drawdown --- it worsens it. The concern was reasonable; the empirical answer is that the current top-N-by-score behaviour is not the failure mode we feared.
    \item \textbf{Regime blindness (T2.1).} Tested empirically. Regime overlay's protection was real in 2011--2015 crises but has disappeared in recent data. Available as a manual toggle; not automatic.
    \item \textbf{Survivorship bias (T1.2).} Accepted as small. The strategy's momentum/quality screens filter delisting candidates long before they liquidate; residual bias is not worth the engineering cost to correct while the strategy proves out in live use.
    \item \textbf{Look-ahead risk (T1.3).} Accepted with spot-checks. Both the regime and clustering modules carry explicit look-ahead unit tests. Factor calculations rely on convention (rolling windows on lagged prices); the risk of a hidden $t{+}1$ dependency is judged low. No formal pinning test.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Open enhancements --- not defects}

Three items remain worth investigating, framed as improvements rather than flaws:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{CAPE-conditional value tilt.} A regime-conditional weight adjustment based on external market valuation (e.g., trailing S\&P 500 CAPE > 30 $\Rightarrow$ tilt value up by 5\%). Distinct from factor timing (see \S\ref{sec:weights}): the signal is market state, not factor performance. Requires CAPE data source verification and its own design note before implementation.
    \item \textbf{Fund-level P/E / P/B in value composite.} Would complete the academic value composite. Blocked on data source: FMP publishes neither at any tier; Morningstar Direct or FactSet would deliver it at institutional cost. Revisit after 3--6 months of live evidence.
    \item \textbf{Score magnitude as expected return.} Currently only rank order is used; exponential rank weighting expresses conviction indirectly. A small A/B against magnitude weighting (softmax on integrated scores) would settle whether the extra information helps. Runtime $\sim$30 seconds; low priority but easy to run.
\end{itemize}

Factor lookback windows (252 / 21 / 60), rebalance cadence (bimonthly), and risk parameters (10\% trailing stop, 12\% entry stop, 5\% drift threshold, 2--15\% weight bounds) are inherited from convention and the pre-2026 backtest and are considered stable operating choices. Sensitivity work on any of these is a discretionary future investigation, not blocking.
