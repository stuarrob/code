\begin{abstract}
Systematic strategy for a leveraged ETF sleeve of TECL (3x Technology), ROM (2x Technology), and NAIL (3x Homebuilders), equal-weighted. Position is scaled from zero to full exposure by two gates and one graduated overlay: (1) a trend gate --- reference index above its 200-day simple moving average, (2) a volatility gate --- 20-day realised volatility below 22\%, and (3) a three-state VVIX overlay --- RISK\_UP / RISK\_MID / RISK\_OFF at 100 and 160 --- that scales exposure to 100\%, 50\%, or 0\% (full exit at extreme VVIX) when the gates are open. Risk-off cash sits in SGOV. Backtested on 10.9 years: Sharpe 1.34, CAGR 31\%, MaxDD -27\% after 5 basis points of transaction cost per rebalance. Live sizing capped at 10\% of net asset value under a Kelly-quarter fractional cap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Strategy Design}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Why leveraged ETFs need active exposure management}

Leveraged ETFs amplify daily index returns by 2x or 3x. Two structural risks:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Volatility decay:} daily rebalancing erodes value in choppy markets. A 3x ETF on an index that moves -10\% then +10\% loses more than the index due to path-dependent compounding.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic drawdowns:} a 30\% index decline maps to 60--80\% loss in a 3x fund, requiring 150--400\% to recover.
\end{itemize}

Systematic scaling of exposure --- from zero to full --- solves both. The strategy uses three signals in a fixed hierarchy: two binary gates that decide whether to hold any exposure at all, and one graduated overlay that decides how much.

\subsection{The trend gate --- 200-day simple moving average}

A \textbf{simple moving average} (SMA) at window $N$ is the arithmetic mean of the last $N$ daily closing prices:

\[
\text{SMA}_{N}(t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} P_{t-i}
\]

The 200-day SMA is a widely-used long-term trend estimator that smooths out short-term noise. When the current price is above the 200-day SMA, the recent 40 weeks of trading have been net upward --- a trending regime. When it is below, the recent history has been net downward --- a broken trend.

The trend gate:
\[
\text{Trend gate} = \begin{cases} \text{OPEN}  & \text{if } P_t^{\text{ref}} > \text{SMA}_{200}(P^{\text{ref}}) \\ \text{CLOSED} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\]

where $P^{\text{ref}}$ is the reference index (XLK for TECL and ROM, ITB for NAIL). We use the reference index, not the leveraged ETF itself, because a leveraged ETF's own SMA is distorted by daily-decay effects.

\subsection{The volatility gate --- 20-day realised vol}

The 20-day annualised realised volatility of the reference index is:

\[
\sigma^{(20)}(t) = \sqrt{252} \cdot \text{std}\left(\{r_{t-19}, \ldots, r_t\}\right)
\]

where $r_i$ is the daily log return. It measures how choppy the last 4 weeks have been.

The volatility gate:
\[
\text{Vol gate} = \begin{cases} \text{OPEN}  & \text{if } \sigma^{(20)}(P^{\text{ref}}) < \theta \\ \text{CLOSED} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\]

with $\theta = 22\%$ for both XLK and ITB. This threshold was validated by walk-forward analysis (Section 3).

\textbf{Combined gate.} Exposure is only allowed when \emph{both} gates are open:
\[
\text{Regime} = \begin{cases} \text{ON}  & \text{if Trend gate = Vol gate = OPEN} \\ \text{OFF} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\]

\subsection{The graduated overlay --- three VVIX-driven exposure states}

When both gates are open, the exposure level is set by VVIX (the CBOE VIX-of-VIX index --- the implied volatility of 30-day VIX options). VVIX is a \emph{leading} stress indicator that spikes days-to-weeks before realised volatility crosses the 22\% threshold.

The final exposure combines all three signals as a product:

\[
\text{Exposure}_t = \underbrace{\mathbb{1}_{\text{Trend gate OPEN}}}_{0 \text{ or } 1} \; \cdot \; \underbrace{\mathbb{1}_{\text{Vol gate OPEN}}}_{0 \text{ or } 1} \; \cdot \; \underbrace{s_{\text{VVIX}}(V_t)}_{\{1.0,\, 0.5,\, 0\}} \; \cdot \; w
\]

where $s_{\text{VVIX}}$ is the VVIX scale factor (1.0, 0.5, or 0) and $w$ is the sleeve NAV cap (10\%). The sleeve exits fully to cash whenever \emph{any} of the three signals says exit: trend broken, or vol high, or VVIX in extreme panic. Otherwise exposure is 100\% or 50\% of the cap depending on VVIX bucket.

\textbf{Why three states and not two or four.} Binary halving (RISK\_UP or RISK\_MID only) gives Sharpe 1.34 but has no explicit exit response to extreme VVIX --- it holds 50\% exposure through a March 2020 event. That is not defensible. Adding RISK\_OFF-VVIX at 160 restores the intuition (extreme uncertainty $\to$ exit) at zero cost to Sharpe (still 1.34). Four or more states adds trigger frequency without adding information --- VVIX's distribution has three natural modes (calm, elevated, panic), not five.

\textbf{Why 160 not 130.} At the 130 level, VVIX drops back below within a week or two roughly 80\% of the time --- most 130 events do not resolve into a full crash. Exiting at 130 costs 0.7 pp of CAGR for no material MaxDD improvement. At 160, only 18 days in 10.9 years qualify, and every one of them was during a genuine tail event (2015 China, 2018 volmageddon, 2020 COVID, 2022 rate cycle, 2025 tariff shock). Exit here is unambiguous.

\subsection{Risk-off cash routing --- SGOV}

When Regime = OFF, sleeve cash is placed in SGOV (iShares 0--3 Month Treasury Bond ETF) to earn T-bill yield (currently $\sim$4\%). Over the extended window, the strategy is in cash 55\% of the time; SGOV routing contributes $\sim$2 percentage points to CAGR at zero drawdown risk.

\subsection{Rebalancing cadence}

Signals evaluated on Friday close. Trades placed on the following Monday morning. Rebalances occur when:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Regime state changes (ON $\leftrightarrow$ OFF)
    \item VVIX state changes (UP $\leftrightarrow$ MID, or entry into RISK\_OFF at VVIX $\geq$ 160)
    \item First Friday of a new calendar month (rebalance to 33/33/33)
    \item Drift band: any leg $> \pm 5$ pp from its target weight
\end{itemize}

\section{Walk-Forward Validation}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{What walk-forward validation is}

The 22\% vol threshold is a hand-picked number. To check whether it was chosen because it happened to work on a particular window, we test it out-of-sample.

The procedure:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Split the data into rolling \textbf{3-year training} + \textbf{1-year test} windows, stepping forward by 1 year.
    \item On each training window, grid-search the vol threshold over $\{0.15, 0.18, 0.20, 0.22, 0.25, 0.28, 0.30, 0.35\}$ and pick the value with the best training-window Sharpe.
    \item Apply that trained threshold to the next 1-year test window --- data the algorithm has not seen.
    \item Roll forward. Chain the test-window returns into one out-of-sample track record.
    \item Compare against a static 22\% baseline.
\end{enumerate}

If 22\% was overfit, letting the algorithm re-pick would beat it. If it is a real regime cutoff, static should hold.

\section{VVIX --- Why the 3-state design?}
%% ============================================================

VVIX is a leading stress indicator. Historical episodes where the VVIX crossed 100 fired days-to-weeks before realised volatility of XLK / ITB crossed 22\%:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{August 2015} (China devaluation) --- VVIX 105 on Aug 21; XLK vol crossed 22\% on Aug 24.
    \item \textbf{February 2018} (volmageddon) --- VVIX 141 on Feb 5; XLK vol crossed on Feb 6.
    \item \textbf{Q4 2018} (Fed hike scare) --- VVIX 115 on Oct 11; realised vol lagged by a week.
    \item \textbf{March 2020} (COVID) --- VVIX 200+ multiple days ahead of the realised-vol spike.
    \item \textbf{September 2022} (rate-hike acceleration) --- VVIX 130 on Sep 26; realised vol lagged by 3--4 days.
    \item \textbf{April 2025} (tariff shock) --- VVIX 108 on Apr 4; realised vol crossed on Apr 8.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Design bake-off --- what we tested}

Fifteen VVIX response designs were compared: binary halving, various stepped configurations, sigmoid smooth responses. All net of 5 bps per rebalance, weekly cadence, 5 pp drift band.

\subsection{Why exit at 160 and not 130}

At the 130 level, VVIX drops back below within a week or two roughly 80\% of the time. Most 130 events do not resolve into a full crash. Exiting at 130 costs 0.7 pp of CAGR for no meaningful MaxDD improvement.

At 160, VVIX is at roughly the 99th percentile. Only 18 days in 10.9 years qualify, and every one of them was during a genuine tail event: 2015 China, 2018 volmageddon, 2020 COVID (VVIX peaked at 208), 2022 rate cycle, 2025 tariff shock. Exit here is unambiguous, and the strategy pays essentially nothing to catch these events because they are so rare.

\subsection{Why not sigmoid or 4+ states}

Sigmoid gives the lowest drawdowns (-18\%) but pays 6--8 pp in CAGR because it scales down exposure during normal periods (VVIX 80--100) when there is no follow-through stress. That is over-fitting to drawdown at the cost of return. A 4-state design (100/130/160 $\to$ 100/50/25/0) matches the recommended 3-state on all metrics but adds one more trigger boundary --- no gain for the extra complexity. Three states (calm / elevated / panic) is the right level of granularity.

\subsection{Kelly-quarter with 10\% NAV cap}

Full Kelly ($f^* = \mu / \sigma^2$) for the recommended stack evaluates to $\sim$620\% of NAV --- absurdly high, as is typical for high-Sharpe backtests. Fractional Kelly is standard practice; the 10\% NAV hard cap binds:

\[
w = \min\left(\text{Kelly-quarter}, \; 0.10 \times \text{NAV}\right) = 10\% \times \text{NAV}
\]

The 10\% cap and Kelly-quarter fractional are deliberately more conservative than the Sharpe would justify. Rationale: (a) live variability against backtest is unknown at this point, (b) the operator's stated posture is a cash-buffered long-only sleeve during the confidence-building phase, (c) the cap can be raised after 12 months of clean live operation.

Because sizing is expressed in \% of NAV and the operator invests continuously, this rule works at any NAV size and adjusts automatically as capital accumulates.

\section{Live Operating Protocol}
%% ============================================================

Full operational detail --- paper trading, phased rollout, alerts, recalibration, interventions --- is specified in \verb|docs/ADR-0002-leveraged-standalone-applet.md|. This section summarises the operator's steady-state workflow.

\subsection{Weekly Friday routine ($\sim$5 minutes)}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Applet pulls latest prices at Friday 3:30 PM local; computes regime state and VVIX state.
    \item If no state changes since last week: log-only, no action, no alert.
    \item If regime state changed (ON $\leftrightarrow$ OFF): Telegram alert; operator opens applet, confirms proposal.
    \item If VVIX state changed (UP $\leftrightarrow$ MID, or entry to RISK\_OFF): Telegram alert; operator confirms proposal.
    \item Any confirmed trade is placed as LMT+GTC on the following Monday morning.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Portfolio backtest bounded by NAIL inception (2015-08). TECL and ROM alone have 16.5 years.
    \item Two-underlying exposure (Tech via XLK, Homebuilders via ITB). The Tech leg uses two vehicles (TECL 3x + ROM 2x) but one underlying. Further diversification must come from outside the leveraged sleeve.
    \item SGOV yield modelled as constant 4\%; live yield varies. Effect on CAGR degrades from $\sim$2 pp to $\sim$1 pp if yield falls below 2\%.
    \item VVIX overlay uses close-to-close prices; intra-day tick spikes above 100 do not trigger --- the trigger is the Friday close.
    \item Correlation with smart-beta uses SPY as a proxy; a full smart-beta simulator would sharpen but not change the sign of the result.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future work}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Standalone applet build.} ADR-0002 spec ready to implement (Streamlit surface, paper trading gate, recalibration cycle, alerts, interventions).
    \item \textbf{Parallel non-equity sleeves.} TMF (3x TLT bonds), EDC (3x EM), JNUG (gold miners) could carry the same signal template calibrated for their vol regimes. Each is a fresh calibration exercise --- deferred to a future ADR.
    \item \textbf{Live tracking vs backtest.} After 12 months live, compare live Sharpe / CAGR / MaxDD to backtest expectation; freeze and review if any diverges by more than the pre-registered band.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic pre-inception NAIL returns} from ITB $\times$ 3 with decay adjustment; would extend backtest depth by 5 years.
\end{itemize}
